## Problems Proportion solves

A B-book prop firm takes the other side of its own traders, so every payout comes out of its own pocket. Nobody wants to pay, and the rulebook grows until nobody has to: consistency rules, news bans, a payout that is always one violation away. The only way out is an account that is actually funded and earns real profit, so that a payout is a share of money that exists rather than a cost the firm absorbs. A blockchain is the best place to run that, because the capital, the rules and the payout are all in the open and none of them depends on the firm's goodwill.

- **Opaque rules and blocked payouts.** Floors, splits and payouts are contract code. Nobody can refuse a payout the protocol has already credited.
- **B-book conflict of interest.** Orders reach the real Hyperliquid book, and the backer's own reserve is the first money a loss consumes. A trader losing principal costs the person who funded it, and nobody is paid out of that loss.
- **Counterparty risk.** Funded accounts are non-custodial, and the payout is a contract call, not a support ticket.
- **High barrier to launching a prop firm.** Proportion supplies the capital, the risk enforcement and the settlement, so a builder does not run a contract stack of their own.
- **Losses that reach the pool.** The floor of every account sits where the backer's reserve runs out, and the risk engine closes the account when it breaches that floor. In the ordinary case LP capital is not touched.

## What each side gets

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Trader">
    - Capital without KYC or artificial rules
    - Orders filled on the real Hyperliquid book with real capital, routed through an exchange proxy that answers faster than Hyperliquid's public endpoint
    - A profit split fixed in the contract at activation and unchangeable afterwards
    - Nobody can refuse the payout: settlement credits your address, and the release call pays that address and no other
    - The account is yours to trade within the protocol's rules, and whoever funded it cannot block a withdrawal
    - A risk engine that only does what the contract allows and never holds your funds
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Builder">
    - Infrastructure for funded accounts: capital from the vault, risk enforcement, and settlement
    - No registration, no minimum stake, no quota. Any wallet holding USDC for the activation charge can fund a trader
    - You post a reserve as first-loss capital, and whatever the trader does not lose comes back to you
    - Revenue from three sources: your share of trader profit, a fixed share of the profit forfeited on a forfeiting close, and whatever margin you price into your own evaluation
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="LP">
    - Yield from three flows: half of every activation premium, half of every subscription, and the vault's residual when an account forfeits. Not fixed, and can be zero
    - Your capital is not first in line for a loss. Each account carries a builder's reserve that is spent before LP principal, though a loss larger than that reserve still reaches you
    - You do not pick builders or traders. You hold a share of the vault's whole book, and `sharePrice` can fall as well as rise
    - The risk engine closes an account when it breaches its floor, which is where the builder's reserve runs out. Only an overshoot past the floor reaches LP capital
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
Losses larger than a builder's reserve do reach LPs. What each flow pays, and what it costs when an account overshoots its floor, is on [Yield and Risks](/vault/yield).
</Note>
