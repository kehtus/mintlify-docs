description: "What activating a funded account costs you, which part of it comes back, and what you choose"

Activation charges you two amounts in one transaction. The buffer `B` comes back when the account closes, less whatever the trader lost and any [vault debt repayment](#vault-debt-repayment). The premium `P` does not come back. You do not fund the trading capital: the vault allocates the account size `A` from LP deposits.

## What you choose at activation

| Parameter | Range | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| Trader | any address | Who the account is issued to |
| Account size `A` | \$10–\$20,000 | Trading capital the vault allocates |
| Trailing drawdown `D` | 5 %–25 % | The trader's floor, and your buffer |
| Profit share | 10 %–70 % | Your cut of each withdrawal and each ordinary close |
| Profit target | optional | If set, no withdrawals; reaching it closes the account on the ordinary split |

Daily drawdown is not a choice: an account you back always has it, a self-funded account never does. See [Protocol Rules](/backers/rules).

### How drawdown sets the floor

`D` sets the starting floor at `A − B`. When the account's equity peak rises above `A`, the floor goes up by 30 % of that gain. After a profit withdrawal completes, the peak resets to `A` and the floor returns to `A − B`.

```text
A = $10,000, D = 10 %, buffer B = $1,000

floor at activation                         $9,000
peak $15,000, 30 % of the $5,000 gain       $10,500
equity strictly below $10,500               the account closes
```

In this example equity can fall \$4,500 from the peak before the account closes: your \$1,000 buffer plus 70 % of the \$5,000 gain. A backed account's daily floor may close it sooner. If the account returns \$10,500, that covers the full principal and your buffer comes back whole, apart from any [vault debt repayment](#vault-debt-repayment). The floor starts a close; it does not guarantee the amount returned, so a sudden price gap or liquidation can still use up part or all of the buffer.

## What you pay

```text
A = account size    D = trailing drawdown you choose, 5 %–25 %

B = A × D       your buffer, refundable
P = A × 2 %     the premium, kept
charge = B + P
```

| Account size `A` | Drawdown `D` | Buffer `B` | Premium `P` | Charged at activation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \$500 | 5 % | \$25 | \$10 | **\$35** |
| \$1,000 | 10 % | \$100 | \$20 | **\$120** |
| \$1,000 | 25 % | \$250 | \$20 | **\$270** |
| \$2,500 | 10 % | \$250 | \$50 | **\$300** |

<Note>
The per-account limit is the lower of 10 % of the vault's assets and \$20,000. With \$25,000 in the vault, for example, that limit is \$2,500. Available capacity can limit activation further. See [How It Works](/vault#allocation-limits).
</Note>

A wider drawdown gives the trader more room and raises your buffer. The premium depends on `A` alone, so it stays the same.

<Note>
A backer always pays the 2 % premium, and the activation opens a 30-day subscription period. A trader funding their own account pays the same 2 % for one prepaid day, 4 % for 7 days or 8 % for 30 days.
</Note>

## What comes back

| What the account returns | Your buffer |
|---|---|
| Its full principal | All of `B` |
| Less than that | `B` minus the shortfall |
| Less than `A − B` | Nothing, and the excess lands on [LPs](/vault/yield) rather than on you |
| A forfeiting close while the vault is in debt | As above, less a capped [vault debt repayment](#vault-debt-repayment) |

So the most you can lose on one account is `B + P`. The refund is credited to your address, and you collect it without anyone's approval.

On the profit side, every profit withdrawal and every ordinary close pays you your profit share, fixed at activation. On a forfeiting close, the profit still in the account is split **half and half** between you and the protocol.

<Warning>
If the account returns `A − B` or less, you lose all of `B` on that account, and profit from your other accounts does not offset it. On a backed account the daily floor closes a losing day at about half of `B`, unless equity drops past the floor before the close is done; see [Protocol Rules](/backers/rules#daily-drawdown).
</Warning>

### Vault debt repayment

If losses beyond other accounts' buffers have left the vault in debt, a forfeiting close (a drawdown breach or the trader's request) lets the vault keep part of your unused buffer instead of returning it. This is a last resort and it is capped: at most 30 cents of each dollar of accepted peak profit above `A`, and never more than the debt or your unused buffer. It is automatic and comes out of your refund, so your loss on one account stays within `B + P`. It cannot be switched off at activation, and no other close repays anything. A worked example is on [Yield and Risks](/vault/yield#vault-debt).

## Where your premium goes

The premium `P` splits **50/50** between the vault and the protocol.

The subscription is **1.5 % of account size every 30 days**, rounded up to a whole USDC, and splits the same way. You receive none of it. It is paid from the account's equity, not from your wallet, and renews only if the account has made at least the fee in profit and its equity stays at or above both floors after paying it.
