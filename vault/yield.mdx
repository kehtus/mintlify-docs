title: "Yield and Risks"
description: "Where LP yield comes from, what a losing account costs an LP, how that loss is repaid, and what can go wrong"
---

The vault receives half of each activation premium and half of each subscription fee. Idle USDC placed in Hyperlend can also earn interest. All of this raises the share price, but it does not guarantee you come out ahead.

Losses can run the other way. Every funded account carries a buffer `B` posted by its backer, and a loss comes out of `B` before it touches LP money. If a loss goes past `B` (for example after a gap, a HyperCore liquidation or slippage on a forced close), the excess lowers the share price. The vault records it as [vault debt](#vault-debt); later closes may repay part of it, with no guarantee.

## What pays the vault

| Flow | What the vault gets |
|---|---|
| Activation premium | **Half** of the premium `P` the backer pays at activation. The protocol takes the other half |
| Subscription | **Half** of each subscription payment. The protocol takes the other half |
| Hyperlend interest | Interest on idle USDC the owner lends to Hyperlend |

## What the vault does not earn

| Flow | Recipient |
|------|-----------|
| Unused buffer | Back to the backer, less any [vault debt](#vault-debt) repayment |
| Profit withdrawn during the account's life or paid at an ordinary close | Trader and backer, at the split set for the account |
| Profit forfeited on a drawdown breach or at the trader's request | Backer and protocol, half each |

## Vault debt

The vault records each loss beyond a buffer as **vault debt**. Recording the debt does not raise the share price: the loss has already lowered it, and the debt itself is not an asset. Only an actual repayment brings money back.

Repayment is a last-resort measure, and a narrow one. It only repays a loss LPs have already taken, it runs automatically at settlement, and it is not guaranteed.

It applies when an account closes on a drawdown breach or at the trader's own request (the two forfeiting closes) and the vault is in debt. The vault then keeps the smallest of three amounts: the debt, the account's unused buffer, and 30 cents of each dollar of accepted peak profit above account size ([how the floor rises](/funded/rules#the-floor)).

The money comes out of the backer's unused buffer, never out of the trader's profit; on a self-funded account the trader is the backer, so it reduces their own refund. Other closes repay nothing, and neither does a close with a profit withdrawal still pending.

```text
account size A = $1,000, buffer B = $100, accepted peak $1,200
30 % of the $200 gain                         $60
vault debt                                    $20
drawdown breach, account returns $955: used $45, unused $55
vault keeps min($20, $55, $60)                $20
backer refund                                 $35
```

A repayment is added to share value evenly over 7 days, so a close does not move the share price at once; a newer repayment restarts the clock for the part not yet added. The current debt is `vaultDebt()` on `MainVault`.

## Risks

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Overshoot below the floor" icon="zap">
    In a sharp move, an account can lose more than its floor allows before the close completes. The risk engine runs offchain, so any delay in closing makes this worse. The backer's buffer covers the loss down to `A − B`; anything below that lowers the share price directly. The vault's half of the activation premium helps absorb it but does not guarantee full coverage. Losses on several accounts from one market move add up, and the buffers of accounts that closed whole do not offset them at once: the loss becomes [vault debt](#vault-debt).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Slow or partial debt repayment" icon="hourglass">
    Only forfeiting closes repay debt, and only up to a cap, so repayment can be slow or partial. New losses can outpace it, and a lower share price may not recover. In the rare case that the vault's assets fall below repayments not yet added to the share price, deposits, exits and new activations stop for up to 7 days; settlements and payout claims continue.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Trader selection" icon="users">
    Backers, not LPs, choose the traders and set each account's terms. A backer needs no registration or approval to activate an account directly, but every activation must fit the vault's limits. A backer's choices reach LPs through the fees its accounts pay and any losses beyond their buffers.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Smart-contract and platform risk" icon="file-code">
    The vault and funded-account contracts have had an [independent audit](/security), but some risk always remains, and the audited code can be upgraded (see governance risk below). The vault also reads equity from HyperCore and settles through Hyperliquid's bridge. If either goes down, closes and settlements are delayed, and any loss from that falls on vault capital.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Hyperlend risk" icon="landmark">
    Idle USDC lent to Hyperlend carries that pool's contract and liquidity risks. It counts toward the share price, but an exit that needs it back fails if the pool cannot pay. Backer buffers and payout money are not lent there.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Vault utilization and LP exit" icon="lock">
    Shares are not locked against open funded accounts. An exit is limited by utilization after it, by free cash in the vault and in Hyperlend, and by a pause. Capital already given to accounts cannot be recalled for a withdrawal, so a full exit may be unavailable until accounts close.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Governance risk" icon="settings">
    The owner, a multisig, can change the risk settings and upgrade the vault's code, and both wait 72 hours in the timelock. Utilization, the per-account ceiling and maximum drawdown have fixed bounds; account sizes and the vault cap have no fixed upper limit. A pause stops LP withdrawals but not payout claims. The owner or relayer can pause, and only the owner can unpause.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
