## Problems Proportion solves

A B-book prop firm is the counterparty to its own traders, so every payout is its loss. Nobody wants to pay, and the rulebook grows until nobody has to: new rules, endless KYC checks. The effort spent fighting abuse lands on honest traders too: rules written for cheats, and refused payouts they earned.

Proportion's funded accounts use real capital and execute orders on HyperCore. MainVault on HyperEVM allocates the principal, and contract settlement divides the resulting profit. Challenges remain simulated evaluations with on-chain escrow; the benefits below describe funded trading.

- **Opaque rules and payout approval.** Contracts define equity floors, profit splits and settlement. A credited payout goes to its recorded recipient without backer approval.
- **Backer exposure to losses.** Orders reach the real Hyperliquid book. Principal losses consume the backer's reserve first; the reserve is risk capital, not protocol revenue.
- **Custody and settlement.** Funded capital is held in isolated accounts, and recipients claim credited payouts through the vault.
- **High barrier to launching a prop firm.** Proportion supplies the capital, the risk enforcement and the settlement, so a backer does not run a contract stack of their own.
- **Losses that reach the pool.** Equity floors trigger closure to limit losses. A floor can rise as the account earns profit; it is not simply the point where the backer's reserve runs out. Losses beyond that reserve reach LP capital.

## What each side gets

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Trader">
    - Access to funded capital under explicit trading and withdrawal rules
    - Orders filled on the real Hyperliquid book with real capital, routed through the exchange proxy
    - A profit split fixed in the contract at activation and unchangeable afterwards
    - Settlement credits your address, and the release call pays that address without backer approval
    - You trade within the account's rules; profit withdrawals depend on its TARGET, trading history and current state
    - A keeper that monitors risk, with contracts checking closure and settlement
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Backer">
    - Infrastructure for funded accounts: capital from the vault, risk enforcement, and settlement
    - Direct backing needs no backer registration or LP stake. You pay the reserve and premium; vault liquidity and account limits still apply. Challenge sales have a separate LP-share requirement
    - You post a buffer as first-loss capital, and whatever the trader does not lose comes back to you
    - Revenue from three sources: your share of trader profit, half of the profit forfeited on a forfeiting close, and whatever margin you price into your own evaluation
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="LP">
    - Revenue from half of each activation premium, half of each subscription, and interest on idle USDC supplied to Hyperlend. Net returns depend on revenue and losses
    - Each account carries a backer's reserve that absorbs principal losses before LP capital. Equity floors trigger closure to limit further losses
    - You do not pick backers or traders. You hold a share of the vault's whole book, and the share price can fall as well as rise
    - A price gap, slippage or delayed close can leave a loss larger than the reserve. LPs bear that excess and the other protocol risks described in Yield and Risks
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
