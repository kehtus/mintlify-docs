---
title: "Yield and Risks"
description: "Where LP yield comes from, what a losing account costs an LP, and what can go wrong"
---

Three flows reach the vault and raise `sharePrice`: half of each activation premium, half of each subscription fee, and the vault's residual on a forfeiting close. All three are paid for the use of LP capital. Never guaranteed, can be zero.

One flow runs the other way. Every funded account carries a reserve `R` posted by its builder, and a principal loss consumes `R` before it touches LP capital. An off-chain risk engine closes the account at its floor, which is where `R` runs out, so in the ordinary case the loss stops there. The part of a loss larger than `R` is `reserveDeficit`, and that lands on you. The buffer is real, it is charged for, and it is finite.

## What pays the vault

| Flow | What the vault gets |
|---|---|
| Activation premium | The builder pays the premium `P` at activation. **Half** stays in the vault and the protocol takes the other half. It is earned before the account places an order |
| Subscription | Each subscription payment splits **50 / 50** between the vault and the protocol. The builder receives none of it |
| Forfeiting-close residual | A forfeiting close pays the trader nothing. The builder takes **40 %** and the protocol **20 %**; the remaining **40 %**, rounding included, stays with LPs |

<Warning>
An ordinary close pays the vault nothing. When an account closes for expiry, cancellation, admin action or a reached target, profit is split between trader and builder by `builderPoolBps` and the vault's share is zero. The residual in the last row exists only on a forfeiting close. Do not model LP return as a cut of trader profit.
</Warning>

## What the vault does not earn

| Flow | Recipient |
|------|-----------|
| Unused reserve, `reserveRefund` | The builder who posted `R`, in full |
| Ordinary close profit | Trader and builder, split by `builderPoolBps` |
| Profit withdrawn during the account's life | Trader and builder, split by `builderPoolBps` |
| Protocol half of premium and subscription | `protocolFeeReceiver` |
| Builder 40 % and protocol 20 % at a forfeiting close | Builder and protocol |

<Note>
Unused reserve returns to the builder. The builder's terms say it plainly: **You pay a reserve to fund the account. Unused reserve is returned when it closes.** It is not vault income at any point, and no part of it reaches LPs.
</Note>

## Where a loss reaches you

The risk engine closes an account when its equity falls below the risk floor. The trailing floor starts at exactly `A − R` and rises only as accepted profit locks in, so the reserve is sized to cover the whole of the designed fall and the engine is meant to act before LP capital is touched. Settlement then compares `Q`, the capital actually recovered from HyperCore, against the principal:

```text
principalLoss   = max(A − Q, 0)
reserveConsumed = min(R, principalLoss)
reserveRefund   = R − reserveConsumed
reserveDeficit  = principalLoss − reserveConsumed
```

`reserveConsumed` is the builder's money. `reserveDeficit` is yours, and it is the only way a closing account can move `sharePrice` down. The fall is exactly `reserveDeficit`.

A deficit appears only when the risk engine cannot close the account at its floor: a gap, a HyperCore liquidation, slippage during a forced close, or a settlement that recovers less than the snapshot. Nothing reimburses it, and the premium already collected on that account is the only sum ever charged against the possibility.

A firm that funds accounts from its own fee revenue has no outside capital and nothing to offer an LP. Here the order of losses is explicit: the backer absorbs the first loss, and the LP absorbs what is left.

## Risks

Placing capital in the vault involves several risks that affect share value and LP yield.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Correlated drawdowns" icon="waves">
    Each overshoot contributes its own `reserveDeficit`, which the premium the vault has already earned is meant to absorb in isolated events. With simultaneous breach closures across many funded accounts, aggregate losses may exceed the pool's accumulated buffer, reducing `sharePrice`, potentially below the deposit's nominal value. Deficits are not netted against the reserves of accounts that closed whole.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Issuance quality" icon="users">
    LPs do not participate in trader selection or activation parameter decisions; these are made by builders. Activation is permissionless: any wallet that can pay `R + P` becomes the builder of record, with no registration and no approval. Deterioration in underwriting quality by any builder affects vault yield in proportion to their share of active issuance.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Smart-contract risk" icon="file-code">
    `MainVault` and `FundedAccount` carry smart-contract risk. The codebase has had an [independent audit](/security), but residual risk cannot be eliminated, and the audited implementation is not frozen. See governance risk below.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Dependency on Hyperliquid and HyperCore" icon="link">
    Vault operations rely on HyperCore data (balances, prices) and on Hyperliquid spot and perp infrastructure. Failures or incorrect behaviour in these components are reflected in vault capital.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Overshoot below the floor" icon="zap">
    In extreme volatility a position may realize a loss below the floor before the risk engine's close completes, and the engine is off-chain, so anything that delays its close widens the overshoot. Everything down to `A − R` is covered by the builder's reserve; the overshoot is `reserveDeficit` and lands on `sharePrice` directly. The vault's half of the activation premium partially absorbs this, but does not guarantee full coverage.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Vault utilization and LP exit" icon="lock">
    Shares are not locked against open funded accounts, for any LP. What limits an exit is the utilization ceiling measured after the burn, EVM cash above the reserve, refund and payout liabilities, and whether the protocol is paused. Allocated capital cannot be recalled to serve a withdrawal, so a full exit may be unavailable until accounts close.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Governance risk" icon="settings">
    The account-size band, the drawdown band, the utilization ceiling, the per-account ceiling and the vault cap are set by the owner in one call. The utilization and per-account ceilings, and the top of the drawdown band, have bounds compiled into the contract; the account-size band and the vault cap do not. The owner can also replace the vault implementation. Neither carries an observation window today. Separately, a pause disables every LP withdrawal path while leaving payout claims working; the owner or the relayer can trigger one, and only the owner can lift it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
