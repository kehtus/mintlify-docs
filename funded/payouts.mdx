---
title: "Profit and Payouts"
description: "How your profit is split, how you withdraw it, and which closures pay nothing"
---

Nobody can refuse your payout. When your account settles, the vault credits your address in its payout ledger, and `claimPayout` sends that balance to that address and to no other. Anyone may call it. There is no approval step, no ticket, and no address the operator can substitute for yours.

What you can lose is the profit itself. Some closures pay the trader nothing. Both halves of that are on this page.

## Your split

One number decides it: `builderPoolBps`, written into your account record at activation, inside a range the contract refuses to leave.

| `builderPoolBps` | Builder | You |
|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 10 % | **90 %** |
| 2000 | 20 % | **80 %** |
| 7000 | 70 % | **30 %** |

Your share is somewhere between 30 % and 90 %, and nothing changes it after activation. The same number governs a withdrawal and an ordinary close.

<Note>
If you were quoted 80 %, that is a plan that set `builderPoolBps` to 2000. It is your plan's number, not the protocol's. Ask the firm issuing your account for its value before you accept it.
</Note>

The split has no third leg. The vault and the protocol take nothing from an ordinary withdrawal or an ordinary close; their revenue is the activation premium and the subscription. On a self-funded account both legs land in the same wallet, so you keep all of it.

## Withdrawing profit

Profit is your current equity above account size. The high-water mark is not the basis. You need at least $1 of it, and either route below.

| Route | Condition |
|---|---|
| Day count | Three profitable checkpoint days, each at least 0.7 % growth. They need not be consecutive |
| Instant | Profit at or above 3 % of account size, with no day count at all |

At the moment you request, the account must also be flat, the subscription active, and the profit target not yet reached.

<Steps>
  <Step title="You request">
    The request is yours alone to make, and the gas is paid for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The profit bridges">
    Your profit moves from HyperCore to HyperEVM.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Anyone claims">
    The claim takes no permissions. Your share transfers to your address; the builder's share is booked to theirs.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
A claim resets your protection. The high-water mark, the daily baseline and the daily peak all return to account size, and the profitable-day count returns to zero. The floor you had ratcheted up is gone.
</Warning>

## What each closure pays

| Why the account closed | Profit still in the account |
|---|---|
| Drawdown breach | Nothing |
| You asked to close | Nothing |
| Subscription expired | Your ordinary split |
| Subscription cancelled | Your ordinary split |
| Admin closure | Your ordinary split |
| Profit target reached | Your ordinary split |

On a forfeiting close your share of the remaining profit is zero, and whatever the account returns above its capital goes to the builder, the protocol and the vault. If it comes back at or below account size there is no profit to divide, and the builder's reserve absorbs the loss before LP capital does.

<Warning>
Closing your own account is a forfeiting close. You can ask only while the account is flat, it closes at whatever equity it has, and it creates no payout entitlement. If you have profit and you want it, withdraw and claim it before you ask to close.
</Warning>

## The one thing a close cannot reach

Profit already accepted into a pending withdrawal is vested. If a close interrupts it, that amount still settles under your ordinary split, whatever the reason for the close turns out to be. A drawdown breach in the next block does not touch it.

So the order of your actions matters more than the size of any single trade. Eligible profit you have requested is yours. Eligible profit you have not requested is not.
